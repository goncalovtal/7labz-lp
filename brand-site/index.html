01 · The story

Faster businesses, and evenings back

Most founders run everything themselves. Their business runs on spreadsheets, inboxes and memory, and the hours leak out in admin, chasing leads and answering the same questions.

7labz is a small team of growth engineers. We find the part of a business that is slow, build the thing that fixes it, and stay until the hours come back. Studios sell hours. We sell speed.

And every so often a founder is building something we cannot stop thinking about. Then we stop billing and take a stake through 7labz Ventures.

  1. Tension

    You run everything

    Spreadsheets, inboxes, memory. The hours leak and you feel it at home.

  2. Turn

    Map, build, ship

    One team finds the leak, builds the system and makes it live in weeks.

  3. Payoff

    Time back

    It runs without you. Weekends off, evenings home on time.

  4. Second act

    Back

    For the right founder, equity instead of an invoice.

04 · Tone of voice

Plain, sure of itself, a little warm

We sound like a senior engineer who likes you: direct, specific, no pitch voice. If a line could appear in any agency's ad, rewrite it.

Short and plain

Short sentences. Everyday words. One idea per line.

Concrete over abstract

Name the real thing: spreadsheets, inboxes, invoices, follow-ups. Never "workflows" or "solutions".

Human outcomes

Sell evenings and weekends, not efficiency gains. Hours are the unit we count in.

"We" and "you"

Talk as the team, to one owner. No "businesses like yours", no third person.

Opinionated, not arrogant

State a view and back it. Admit limits: "When we are full, we are full."

No unearned claims

No client names, results, stats or history until they are real and signed off.

Sentence case, no full stops

Headlines, ledes and buttons drop the final period. Body copy in longer formats can keep them.

No hype, no emoji

No exclamation marks, no rocket emoji, no "AI-powered" as a selling point. AI is something we ship, not a badge.

09 · Imagery and devices

A white horse, rendered in glyphs

The signature image is a horse at full gallop, always drawn in monospace characters so it reads like code becoming something alive. Never use it as a photo. It carries speed and strength without saying either. Use it, the pixel-cut buttons and the green highlight. Skip stock photos of laptops, handshakes and dashboards.

Glyph rendering

The horse is only ever shown dithered into the glyphs . : - + * # %, lavender or green on indigo. In motion it loops gently, never flashes.

Apply for a slotCheck availability

Pixel-cut buttons

Square buttons with three-step pixel corners. Indigo on light, mist on dark. Never rounded pills.

Green highlight

Mark the one phrase that matters, like a highlighter pen. Once per frame, at most.

10 · Before an ad goes live

The checklist

  • One pillar per ad, one idea per frame
  • Headline under eight words, no full stop
  • Indigo or white ground, lavender or white type
  • Green only as a highlight or small mark
  • Logo lowercase, lavender on indigo, with clear space
  • Horse in glyphs only, never the photo
  • CTA is "Apply for a slot" or "Check availability"
  • No client names, stats or results we cannot prove
  • Read it aloud. If it sounds like a pitch, cut it

11 · Not yet signed off

Do not run these yet

The site flags this copy as draft. Hold it out of ads until the founders confirm.

Time-back numbers

"We take 60% off your plate" and the Day 1 → Week 4 timeline are placeholders until the team confirms what it can promise.

Slot scarcity

"Q4 2026: 2 of 3 slots taken" is hard-coded. Only use slot counts if they are true on the day the ad runs.

Service list

Service names, descriptions and sub-items are marked draft in the source.

Draft. Safe to quote lines in the bank above; avoid quoting new passages.
